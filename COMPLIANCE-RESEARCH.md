## 1. Industry practice (what the service is and isn't)

| Source | Finding | How it shaped the site |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. EPA, *Should You Have the Air Ducts in Your Home Cleaned?* (EPA-402-K-97-002) — epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/should-you-have-air-ducts-your-home-cleaned | EPA does not recommend routine cleaning, only cleaning as needed. Situations where cleaning may be considered: substantial visible mold, vermin infestation, ducts clogged with dust/debris or particles released from supply registers. Duct cleaning has never been shown to prevent health problems. No chemical biocides are EPA-registered for use in internally insulated ducts; biocides/sealants should only be considered after proper cleaning. Wet or moldy insulation should be removed and replaced, and the moisture cause fixed first. | FAQ "Why is it important?" and "How often?" quote EPA's position and link to it. No health, allergy or air-quality guarantees anywhere. No chemical treatments in scope. Mold → specialist referral. |
| NADCA (National Air Duct Cleaners Association), *ACR, The NADCA Standard* (2025 edition, free at acrstandard.nadca.com) | Industry standard for assessment, cleaning and restoration; based on source removal (mechanical agitation + vacuum collection); NADCA suggests cleaning every 3–5 years for many homes. | Service method described as mechanical source removal. The FAQ presents NADCA's 3–5 year suggestion as an *industry suggestion*, alongside EPA's "as needed" position — not as a requirement. The site does **not** claim NADCA membership or ACR compliance. |
| State attorneys general (e.g. Michigan AG, Dec 2023; Ohio AG) and consumer reporting | Common duct-cleaning scams: very low "whole house" teaser prices, bait-and-switch upselling, fake mold "discoveries", pressure to buy on the spot, false NADCA membership claims. | Clear starting prices with conditions; written quote before work; no additional work without approval; specialist issues shown to the customer with no on-the-spot pressure; no invented credentials. |

## 2. Advertising & pricing

| Rule | Relevance | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **FTC Act §5** — unfair or deceptive acts or practices | Claims must be truthful and substantiated (incl. health and performance claims). | No health/IAQ/energy claims; no invented awards, years, reviews, ratings or certifications. |
| **FTC Guides Against Bait Advertising, 16 CFR Part 238** | Advertised offers must be bona fide; advertisers shouldn't disparage or refuse the advertised offer to switch customers to something pricier. | "Starting at" prices with stated conditions; Terms §5 commits to genuinely offering each package and not discouraging it. |
| **FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing, 16 CFR Part 233** | Former-price comparisons must reflect real prior prices. | No "was/now" prices, discounts or countdown timers. |
| **FTC Guides Concerning Use of Endorsements and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 255)** and the FTC rule on fake reviews (16 CFR Part 465, 2024) | Fake or misleading reviews/testimonials are prohibited. | No testimonials or ratings. Add only genuine ones later. |

## 3. Cancellation & refund obligations

- **FTC Cooling-Off Rule, 16 CFR Part 429** — sales of $25+ made at the buyer's home (or other non-permanent seller locations) generally carry a 3-business-day right to cancel, with required oral notice, written notice and two cancellation forms. The Rule has exemptions, and whether it applies depends on how and where the sale is made. → Terms §11 acknowledges the right "where it applies"; **attorney to confirm applicability**, especially if quotes or upgrades are agreed on site.
- **State home-solicitation / home-improvement laws** may add rights or contract formalities. → Flagged for review.
- No federal rule sets a general refund period for services. → Terms §13 gives a good-faith remedy (re-clean or refund for the affected work) and states that statutory rights are not limited.

## 4. Liability

- Many states restrict how far consumer contracts can limit liability for negligence, and some limits are void as unconscionable or against public policy. → Terms §20 limits liability only "to the fullest extent permitted by law", never excludes liability for death/personal injury from negligence, fraud, gross negligence or wilful misconduct, and keeps the customer's statutory rights. **Attorney review required.**
- No arbitration clause or class-action waiver has been added (these need legal advice).
- No insurance, licensing or bonding claims have been made; a placeholder remains for the owner to confirm or delete.

## 5. Privacy

| Law | Applies when | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **CCPA/CPRA (California)** | A for-profit business doing business in California that meets a threshold, e.g. more than $26,625,000 annual gross revenue (2025 CPI-adjusted figure from the California Privacy Protection Agency), buying/selling/sharing personal information of 100,000+ California consumers or households, or earning 50%+ of revenue from selling/sharing personal information. | Unlikely for a small local business, but depends on facts. Policy describes rights generally and commits to consider any reasonable request. |

| **Cookie consent** | Required in some jurisdictions for non-essential cookies/trackers. | The site sets **no cookies**, loads **no third-party scripts**, and self-hosts fonts, so no consent banner is needed. Must be revisited if analytics, pixels or widgets are added. |

The Privacy Policy matches what the site actually does: no forms, no analytics, no cookies, no tracking; contact happens through the visitor's own phone/email app; the hosting provider processes standard server logs.
